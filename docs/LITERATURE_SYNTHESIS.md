### 1.2 Key Insight: Feature Importance Convergence

Three independent studies (ours, SW Pacific XGBoost, WAF NN study) all converge on the same conclusion:
**Scalar SHIPS-style environmental features capture the dominant RI signal.**

- SW Pacific XGBoost: Longitude, initial intensity, latitude, RH at 850hPa
- WAF NN study: CNN on satellite imagery adds only small gain over SHIPS scalars
- Our logistic model: Uses wind, pressure, lat/lon, shear, RH, T600, winds aloft

This is remarkable because it means:
1. Complex architectures (CNNs, transformers) provide diminishing returns for RI
2. Simple, interpretable models can achieve competitive RI skill
3. The bottleneck is not model complexity but data quality and physical understanding

### 1.3 LLM vs. Structured Models for RI

Our most novel finding: **LLMs catastrophically fail at RI detection.**

The WAF NN study provides a useful analogy. Their "NN without SHIPS data" (using only satellite images) performed "surprisingly well" for RI prediction. This is analogous to our LLM approach, which uses only unstructured text descriptions without structured environmental data.

However, there's a crucial difference:
- Their CNN without SHIPS data: trained specifically on RI classification
- Our LLMs: general-purpose models prompted to make hurricane forecasts

The LLMs haven't been trained on RI-specific signals, explaining their regression-to-mean bias. They predict "typical" hurricane behavior rather than the rare, extreme events that define RI.
